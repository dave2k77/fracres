\begin{abstract}
\noindent First population study on ds002778 (commits \texttt{5523670},
\texttt{a9e599c}, \texttt{04bb8de}): for every subject $\times$ session
$\times$ \{Fz, Cz, Pz\}, the best-fit generative mechanism $(\alpha, H,
\lambda)$ was grid-searched against the channel's (DFA-$H$, spectral-$\beta$)
statistics, with the fBm drive promoted to a first-class citizen of
\texttt{fracres.drivers} and the config system. Headline: the phantom class
fits \emph{all} 44 usable recordings well; the fractional order $\alpha$ is
near-Markovian ($\approx 0.92$--$0.94$) and group-invariant, so the LRD lives
in the inferred drive, not the medium; and the two candidate effects --- PD
vs.\ control at Cz, and medication OFF$\to$ON whitening --- are consistent in
direction across model-based and model-free estimators but underpowered at
this $n$. Two analysis failures (a provenance trap and a grid-boundary
artifact) are recorded with their resolutions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Design}

All 31 ds002778 subjects (15 PD $\times$ ses-off/ses-on, 16 HC $\times$
ses-hc), channels Fz/Cz/Pz, recordings resampled to 64\,Hz and cropped to a
uniform 120\,s (7{,}680 samples, so estimator finite-sample biases are
comparable across subjects and sessions). Grid: $\alpha \in \{0.85, 0.9,
0.95, 1.0\}$, $H \in [0.15, 0.80]$ at $0.05$ spacing, $\lambda = 1$, 3 seeds
per point with common random numbers; objective = Euclidean distance on
(DFA-$H$, $\beta$), model vs.\ data. v2 excluded two ON sessions (below) and
extended the $H$ grid downward; incremental resumable CSV
(\texttt{outputs/off\_on\_mechanisms\_v2.csv}, 132 rows $=$ 44 recordings
$\times$ 3 channels).

\section{Results}

\paragraph{1. The phantom class is adequate for real EEG.}
Objectives are $0.046$--$0.092$ across \emph{all} groups and channels ---
the same scale as the near-exact single-subject fit. An fBm-driven
contractive fractional reservoir reproduces the (DFA-$H$, $\beta$) signature
of real resting-state scalp EEG; this validates the forward-modelling
premise of the whole project at population scale.

\paragraph{2. The medium is near-Markovian; the LRD lives in the drive.}
Best-fit $\alpha = 0.92$--$0.94$ with no group or session differences
($\pm 0.05$--$0.06$ spread, means indistinguishable). Best-fit $H$ does the
work: $\approx 0.52$--$0.63$ depending on site and group. Within this model
class, inter-subject and inter-session variability in cortical LRD is
attributed to the \emph{driving process's} Hurst exponent, not to the
medium's fractional order. (Caveat: $\alpha$ near the grid boundary --- the
class may simply not reward $\alpha \neq 1$ strongly; see open threads.)

\paragraph{3. PD vs.\ control: reduced persistence at Cz (suggestive).}
At Cz, HC data DFA-$H = 1.41$ vs.\ PD-off $1.35$ (Mann--Whitney
$p = 0.046$); model-based best-$H$ agrees in direction ($0.631$ vs.\ $0.577$,
$p = 0.078$). Fz and Pz show nothing ($p \geq 0.10$). Model-free and
model-based estimators converging at the same electrode is the strongest
internal evidence; but $p = 0.046$ does not survive a 3-channel Bonferroni
($\alpha^\ast \approx 0.017$), so this is \emph{suggestive}, not
confirmatory.

\paragraph{4. Medication OFF$\to$ON: consistent whitening, underpowered.}
Paired across the 13 usable PD subjects: $\Delta H = -0.01 / -0.06 / -0.04$
at Fz/Cz/Pz (Wilcoxon $p = 0.89 / 0.12 / 0.18$); model-free $\Delta$DFA at
Cz $= -0.05$ ($p = 0.068$). Every channel points the same way --- dopamine
\emph{whitens} the effective drive (reduces persistence) --- but $n = 13$
paired subjects cannot resolve an effect of this size.

\section{Failures encountered and how they were resolved}

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.6em,style=nextline]
  \item[The provenance trap (v1's ``5$\times$ worse ON fits'').]
  v1 showed ON-session objectives $\approx 0.35$ vs.\ $\approx 0.07$ for
  everything else, and ON data statistics with tripled variance. The cause
  was not medication: \texttt{sub-pd6} and \texttt{sub-pd16} ON sessions are
  reconstructed from \emph{preprocessed EEGLAB .mat} files (flagged in
  \texttt{participants.tsv} notes), and the preprocessing destroys the $1/f$
  structure --- their ON data $\beta$ is \emph{negative} ($-0.42$, $-0.24$)
  vs.\ $\approx 1.6$ for everyone else. Excluding them, ON fits are as good
  as OFF. \emph{Resolution:} the exclusion is encoded in the study script
  with the reason, not applied post-hoc. \emph{Lesson:} read the provenance
  column \emph{before} running 46 recordings; and a fit residual is a
  data-quality detector --- the model flagged the bad rows before we did.

  \item[The grid-boundary artifact (v1's ``ON regime extends below 0.45'').]
  In v1 ($H \geq 0.45$), 31--46\% of ON best fits piled up at the grid
  minimum, suggesting the ON regime lived below the grid. v2 extended $H$
  down to $0.15$: \emph{zero} best fits at the new floor; the ON distribution
  settles in $[0.35, 0.70]$. The ``downward pull'' was truncation, not
  signal. \emph{Lesson:} a pile-up at a grid edge is always a grid
  diagnostic first and a finding second; extend before interpreting.

  \item[Two test-caught subtleties in the fBm driver.]
  (i) $\mathrm{diff} \circ \mathrm{cumsum}$ is \emph{not} bit-identical:
  floating-point cancellation at cumsum magnitude $O(70)$ leaves a
  $\sim 10^{-14}$ residual --- assert \texttt{atol=1e-10}, not equality.
  (ii) z-scoring is \emph{affine} ($(x-\mu)/\sigma$), so the increments are
  pure-scaled but the trace is not --- the ratio check belongs on
  \texttt{diff}. Both were caught by tests, not inspection.
\end{description}

\section{Validation patterns that earned trust}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{Model-based / model-free cross-validation.} Every candidate
        effect was computed both from the best-fit mechanism ($H$) and
        directly from the data (DFA-$H$, $\beta$). Both candidate effects
        (Cz group difference, OFF$\to$ON whitening) appear in both views with
        the same sign --- agreement between an inverse-problem estimate and a
        direct estimator is much stronger evidence than either alone.
  \item \textbf{Uniform-length crops.} Cropping every recording to 120\,s
        made estimator finite-sample bias a constant across subjects and
        sessions, removing a confound that the raw files (192--213\,s) would
        have introduced.
  \item \textbf{Versioned grids with named artifacts.} Keeping v1's CSV and
        script behaviour reproducible made the boundary artifact
        demonstrable rather than anecdotal.
\end{itemize}

\section{Open threads}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{Confirmation cohort.} Both candidate effects need $n \gg 13$:
        OpenNeuro \texttt{ds008768} (312 participants, 358 resting-state PD/HC
        recordings) is the obvious next dataset; the ingestion module is
        dataset-agnostic BIDS, so this is a mirror + config change.
  \item \textbf{$\alpha$ at the boundary.} Best-fit $\alpha \approx 1$
        everywhere either means the medium is Markovian or that the objective
        is insensitive to $\alpha$ near 1 at this $T$. A sensitivity study
        (profile the objective along $\alpha$ at fixed best $H$) would
        distinguish these.
  \item \textbf{Multi-channel readout / spatial model.} Channels are
        currently fit independently; the neural-field variant with a
        leadfield-like readout (Next 2) would couple them spatially and
        likely sharpen the Cz effect.
  \item \textbf{Avalanche/criticality statistics} have not yet entered the
        objective; adding them (Next 3 machinery) tests whether the phantom
        matches more than second-order structure.
\end{itemize}
